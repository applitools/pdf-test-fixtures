000Volunteer Notes for the Harbourside Lifeboat Museum
11101The Harbourside Lifeboat Museum occupies the old boathouse at the end of the east pier and is run entirely by volunteers. These notes are for anyone taking a shift on the door, in the boat hall or in the workshop, and they assume no knowledge of boats, of museums or of the sea. Everything a volunteer needs in order to open the building, look after its visitors and close it again is written here, and where it is not, the person to ask is named.
22302The museum exists because the station moved to the new slipway in 1994 and the town declined to let the boathouse be sold. The building had housed a lifeboat since 1867, and the crew lists on the wall inside the door run without a gap from that year to the last launch. A trust was formed, the council granted a lease, and the first volunteers opened the doors the following summer with a single boat, a borrowed till and a hand-lettered sign. Most of what is described in these notes grew out of that first season.
3551324There are three boats on display and one in the workshop. The oldest is a pulling and sailing boat of 1902, restored over eleven winters; the largest is the motor lifeboat that served here from 1951 to 1978; the smallest is an inshore inflatable that most visitors recognise at once. The boat in the workshop is a private restoration on loan, and the owner works on it on Thursdays. Visitors may watch from the rail but may not enter the workshop, however friendly the invitation sounds.
4671325Opening up takes two people and about twenty minutes. One unlocks the side door, disarms the alarm within the sixty seconds it allows, and switches on the hall lights from the panel by the stairs.
